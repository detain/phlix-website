About Quantum Stream

Philosophy

In quantum mechanics, the act of observation changes the state of what is being observed. A particle exists in multiple states simultaneously until measured — a probability cloud of maybe — at which point the wave function collapses into a single definite outcome.

Quantum Stream applies this profound idea to media. Your library contains every title in superposition: watched and unwatched, favorite and forgotten, queued and completed. The moment you hover over an item, you observe it — the wave function collapses. The content becomes real in a new way.

This isn't just a metaphor. The architecture of Quantum Stream is designed around state management, probability, and the observer effect. Every interaction is a measurement. Every measurement changes the state.

Frequently asked questions

How is this different from Plex or Jellyfin?
While functionally similar, Quantum Stream treats media organization as a quantum mechanical system. The observer effect is not a metaphor but a design principle. The interface reflects this with blur-on-hover effects, probability cloud aesthetics, and wave function collapse animations.
Does the quantum theme affect performance?
No. The quantum theme is purely visual. The underlying media serving, transcoding, and streaming infrastructure is identical to standard implementations.
Why IBM Plex fonts?
IBM Plex Mono and IBM Plex Sans were chosen for their technical precision and excellent legibility at small sizes. The monospace for headings evokes laboratory instrumentation readouts — the aesthetic of scientific measurement.
Is the relay feature secure?
All relay traffic is end-to-end encrypted. We cannot see your media library, only route the encrypted tunnel. No media data transits our servers in plaintext.
Can I self-host the relay infrastructure?
Yes. The relay component is open source and can be deployed on your own servers. This gives you full control over the tunnel infrastructure while maintaining the same client experience.
Is there a mobile app?
Yes. iOS, tvOS, and Android apps are available. The mobile apps support offline downloads, background sync, and full feature parity with the web interface.
