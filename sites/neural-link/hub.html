Phlix Hub

Your server, anywhere. No port forwarding, no Dynamic DNS, no third-party tunnel.

What the Hub does

Phlix Hub is a directory and reverse-tunnel relay. When your server starts, it registers with the Hub and opens an outbound tunnel. The Hub brokers the connection, so you can reach your server from anywhere — even if it's behind a NAT, CGNAT, or a restrictive firewall.

Think of the Hub as a meeting point: your phone checks in, your server checks in, and the Hub connects them. Nothing is proxied through the Hub's servers — the traffic flows directly between your devices once the tunnel is established.

Reverse-tunnel relay

Your server initiates an outbound connection to the Hub. Inbound connections from your clients are routed through this tunnel. No inbound ports need to be opened.

NAT traversal

CGNAT, double-NAT, carrier-grade firewalls — these don't matter when your server makes the first connection outward. The Hub works in situations where UPnP and port forwarding don't.

Use the public Hub or self-host

The public Hub at hub.phlix.dev is free to use. Sign in with your GitHub account, add your server, and you're done.

If you need more control, deploy your own Hub. The phlix-hub repository is open source (MPL-2.0), and the docs cover self-hosting, user management, and authentication configuration.

phlix-hub Cloud directory + reverse-tunnel relay (MPL-2.0)

Hub mode in clients

Every Phlix client supports Hub mode. Enable it, sign in once, and the client discovers all of your registered servers automatically — whether you're on your home network or on a friend's WiFi across town.

Clients that support Hub mode: Roku, Samsung Tizen, Windows, Mobile (iOS + Android).

  • Hub mode is optional — you can always connect directly to a server on your LAN
  • Bandwidth for tunneled traffic depends on your server's upload speed and the Hub's capacity
  • The Hub does not store or cache your media — it's only a signaling relay
