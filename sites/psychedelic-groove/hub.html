Phlix Hub

Sign in once. Reach any of your servers — from anywhere, through any NAT.

What the Hub does

Most home networks sit behind Carrier-Grade NAT or a firewall with no way to open ports. Phlix Hub solves this with a reverse-tunnel relay.

When your server starts, it opens an outbound connection to the Hub and keeps it alive. When you open any Phlix client and sign in, the Hub routes traffic through that connection — no ports opened, no Dynamic DNS needed.

Self-host or use the public Hub

The public Hub at hub.phlix.dev is free to use. If you want full control, run your own Hub on any VPS — it's a single PHP process with a WebSocket relay.

Self-hosting the Hub also means your tunnel traffic never touches a third-party server, keeping your library truly private even when you're remote.

Hub mode in the clients

Every Phlix client — Roku, Samsung Tizen, Windows, Mobile — has a Hub toggle in settings. Enable it, sign in once, and your client connects to whatever server you've registered with the Hub. Switch servers any time from the same menu.

The relay is the magic

The Hub uses a simple relay protocol: client → Hub → your server, with session tokens that expire and rotate. The Hub never stores your credentials or inspects your media streams — it's a dumb pipe with a directory.

Source is at github.com/detain/phlix-hub, licensed MPL-2.0.
