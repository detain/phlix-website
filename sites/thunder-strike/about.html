About Thunder Strike

Electrical storm powering your media. Built by power users, for power users.

The Story

Thunder Strike was born from a simple idea: media servers should feel powerful. Not just functional, not just useful — actually electric. We wanted something that crackles with energy when you interact with it, something that makes organizing your media feel like commanding a tesla coil.

Built on the Phlix platform, Thunder Strike brings that electric vision to life. Every interaction generates voltage. Every discovery is a small lightning strike illuminating something new. The storm is always there, waiting to strike.

Open source. No subscriptions. No cloud dependency. The power stays in your hands.

Electric FAQ

Is Thunder Strike like Plex / Jellyfin / Emby?

Yes — same job, different stack. Thunder Strike is built in PHP 8.3+ on Workerman, ships with a versioned plugin contract, and includes a hub for accessing remote servers behind NAT without a third-party tunnel.

Do I need to expose my server to the internet?

No. Run Thunder Strike on your LAN and use the Phlix Hub's reverse-tunnel relay to reach it from your phone or Roku at a friend's house. You can self-host the hub, or use the public one.

What formats are supported?

Anything FFmpeg can read. Direct play when the client supports it; transcoded HLS otherwise. Per-device quality profiles pick the right bitrate automatically.

Is there a mobile app?

Yes — React Native, available on iOS and Android. Currently in beta.

Can I write plugins?

Yes. Implement LifecycleInterface, ship a manifest, drop it in the plugins directory. See phlix-plugin-example for the smallest working starter.

What's the license?

Thunder Strike and the Hub are MPL-2.0 — open source, and you can build on them commercially. If you modify a file, that file stays open; anything you add alongside it is yours. The shared libraries, plugins, and clients are MIT so you can build on them freely.
