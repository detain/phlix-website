Phlix Hub

Access your media server from anywhere — without opening ports.

What the Hub Does

Your TV at Home

Your Roku or Samsung TV, connected to your home server on your LAN.

→

Phlix Hub

Reverse-tunnel relay. Your server calls out; the Hub keeps the connection open.

←

Your Phone

Check in on your server, browse your library, cast to your TV — from anywhere.

When your server is behind a NAT or firewall, traditional port forwarding is complex and insecure. The Hub uses an outbound tunnel — your server connects to the Hub, and the Hub routes traffic back through that established connection.

Two Ways to Use the Hub

Public Hub

Zero setup. Point your server at hub.phlix.dev, create an account, and you're done. Your tunnel traffic routes through our infrastructure.

Recommended for most users

Self-Hosted

Run phlix-hub on your own VPS. All tunnel traffic stays on infrastructure you control. Open source, MPL-2.0.

View on GitHub

Hub Mode in Clients

All four native clients support Hub mode — sign in once, see all your registered servers.

Roku

Hub mode enabled. Browse your full library and cast to any discovered DLNA device.

Samsung Tizen

Hub mode enabled. Sign in via the Hub to access your remote servers.

Windows

Hub mode enabled. System tray icon for quick server switching.

Mobile (beta)

Hub mode enabled. Offline downloads still work when disconnected; sync on reconnect.

Ready to access your media anywhere?

Start with the public Hub or self-host — your choice.
