Phlix Hub

Sign in once. Reach your server from anywhere. No port forwarding, no third-party tunnel, no trade-off between security and accessibility.

What the Hub does

Reverse-tunnel relay

When your Phlix server starts, it opens an outbound connection to the Hub. That connection stays alive and becomes a relay channel. When you open a client and request your server, the Hub routes the request down that already-open channel — even if your server is behind a CG-NAT, a firewall, or a consumer router with no way to port-forward.

Cloud directory

The Hub maintains a registry of your servers. Sign in once on any client, and the Hub tells your client where to find you. Add a server, give it a name, and it appears on every client you connect — on your LAN or across the planet.

HOW IT LOOKS IN PRACTICE 
# Server: starts, connects to Hub
phlix-server --hub=https://hub.phlix.io

# Client: sign in once, see your server
# → "Living Room Server" (LAN)
# → "Parents' House Server" (via Hub relay)
⚔

Two ways to use it

Use the public hub

Free to use. hub.phlix.io is operated by the Phlix project. No account required beyond what your server uses to authenticate.

Run phlix-server --hub=https://hub.phlix.io and you're connected.

Self-host the Hub

Deploy your own phlix-hub instance. Full control over the relay infrastructure, no data leaves your hardware.

Hub mode in clients lets you point at any Hub URL, including your own.

⚔

Hub mode in clients

Every Phlix client knows how to reach the Hub. In settings, you choose:

  • ▶ Automatic — connect to any server on your LAN first; if not found, try the Hub
  • ▶ Hub only — never probe LAN, always go through the relay
  • ▶ LAN only — never contact the Hub; server must be reachable locally
  • ▶ Custom Hub — point at your own self-hosted Hub URL

Ready to set up your server with Hub?

Self-host or use the public Hub. Either way, no port forwarding needed.
