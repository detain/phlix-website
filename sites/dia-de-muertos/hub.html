The Distance

The hub is the bridge — a reverse-tunnel relay that reaches your server from anywhere, so Aunt Maria across the country can watch the same film at the same time as the family in the living room.

What the Hub does

Most home networks sit behind a router with no way in from the outside. The Hub's reverse-tunnel relay solves this: your server opens an outbound connection to the hub, and the hub keeps it alive. When you connect to the hub from your phone or Roku, the hub routes the request through that outbound connection to your server — no port forwarding, no dynamic DNS, no exposed services.

The hub also acts as a directory: you sign in once, and the hub knows which of your servers are online and how to reach them. If you run multiple servers — one at home, one at a parent's house — they all appear in the same place.

Self-host or use the public hub

The public hub at hub.phlix.io is free to use for personal setups. It is maintained by the project and requires no configuration on your part beyond entering your hub URL in the client settings.

If you need control, privacy, or higher throughput, self-host the hub on any VPS. It is a separate PHP application — phlix-hub on GitHub — with its own configuration and no dependency on the public relay.

phlix-hub on GitHub Hub admin docs

Hub mode in clients

Every Phlix client supports Hub mode. In the settings, enter your hub URL (or leave it at the public hub) and sign in with your Phlix account. The client will automatically discover your registered servers through the hub and present them as if you were on the same network.

SyncPlay works through the hub as well — the NTP time sync messages flow through the relay, so family members across the country stay perfectly in sync just as if they were on the same LAN.
