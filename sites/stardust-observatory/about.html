Our Story

Three rooms and a hearth: what Phlix believes, what its licence actually permits, how to join the work — and then Meridian’s answering service, where the visitors’ letters are read out.

The study

First room

Philosophy

A library you rent is a sky someone else can close. Phlix is self-hostable end to end — 100% self-hostable, and your library never leaves your hardware unless you say so. The server is PHP 8.3+ on Workerman 5.x; metadata comes from TMDB, TVDB, Fanart.tv and your own local NFO files with a 24-hour cache; and the decision about who may look through the eyepiece is yours — up to five profiles per user, 4- or 6-digit PINs, and a rating filter from G to NC-17.

It is also deliberately unglamorous underneath. Nothing here is magic: a scanner, a transcoder, a relay and a plugin loader, each doing one legible job.

Second room

License

Phlix Server and the Hub are MPL-2.0 — open source, and you can build on them commercially. If you modify a Phlix file, that file stays open; anything you add alongside it is yours. The shared libraries, plugins, and clients are MIT so you can build on them freely.

Meridian’s answering service

Letters from the suggestion box

Meridian — the brass armillary sphere that keeps this place company — answers the letters visitors leave. The answers are the project’s, word for word; only the handwriting is its own.

Is Phlix like Plex / Jellyfin / Emby?
Yes — same job, different stack. Phlix is built in PHP 8.3+ on Workerman, ships with a versioned plugin contract, and includes a hub for accessing remote servers behind NAT without a third-party tunnel. Same sky, a different instrument.
Do I need to expose my server to the internet?
No. Run Phlix on your LAN and use the Phlix Hub’s reverse-tunnel relay to reach it from your phone or Roku at a friend’s house. You can self-host the hub, or use the public one. The dome opens outward, never inward.
What formats are supported?
Anything FFmpeg can read. Direct play when the client supports it; transcoded HLS otherwise. Per-device quality profiles pick the right bitrate automatically. Light is light, whatever glass it passed through.
Is there a mobile app?
Yes — React Native, available on iOS and Android. Currently in beta. A pocket eyepiece, still being ground.
Can I write plugins?
Yes. Implement LifecycleInterface, ship a manifest, drop it in the plugins directory. See phlix-plugin-example for the smallest working starter. Every observatory ends up with instruments it built itself.
What’s the license?
Phlix Server and the Hub are MPL-2.0 — open source, and you can build on them commercially. If you modify a Phlix file, that file stays open; anything you add alongside it is yours. The shared libraries, plugins, and clients are MIT so you can build on them freely. Charts are for copying. That is what charts are for.
