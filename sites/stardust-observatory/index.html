The precise term: aperture

An aperture is the opening that admits light to a telescope — the wider it is, the more of the sky you gather. On this site it is only a figure of speech for how much of your library a feature can take in; nothing inside Phlix is named after one.

From the observer’s journal

Why settle for the light we’re given?

A rented library is someone else’s sky: it changes without warning, and one night the thing you loved is simply gone. Phlix hands the dome back to you — your hardware, your catalogue, your rules about who may look through the eyepiece.

“What we seek is always older and larger than we imagined.” — the observatory’s founding fable

  • 100% self-hostable — your library never leaves your hardware unless you say so
  • Native clients on Roku, Samsung Tizen, Windows, Mobile, plus any DLNA device
  • Real-time SyncPlay with NTP-style time sync so movie night actually stays in sync
  • Multi-source metadata: TMDB, TVDB, Fanart.tv, and local NFO with 24-hour cache
  • Adaptive HLS streaming, FFmpeg transcoding, per-device quality profiles
  • Live TV with DVR + EPG guide integration
  • Plugin system with a versioned manifest contract
The precise term: NTP-style time sync

Plainly: every screen agrees on what “now” means. Phlix measures the round-trip delay to each device several times and takes a weighted mean of the offsets — the same trick the internet’s clocks use — so a pause in one room lands on the same frame in the next.

Open your own dome in three unhurried steps. The first is one line in a terminal. 

git clone https://github.com/detain/phlix-server.git
cd phlix-server
composer install
php start.php start

Requires PHP 8.3+ and Workerman 5.x; FFmpeg for transcoding.
