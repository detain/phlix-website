The Phlix story

Every petal, electric.

Deep in a garden that only blooms after midnight, every flower carries its own light. Orchids pulse electric violet. Roses burn neon pink against a sky so black it swallows sound. Fireflies drift between petals like living sparks of gold, and even the leaves exhale a faint green luminescence.

Phlix was born from that image: the conviction that watching a film, an episode, or a live concert should feel like stepping into a place that cannot exist in daylight. A place where beauty is electric, darkness is velvet, and every press of play opens a garden gate into the extraordinary.

What Phlix does

Glow at the heart of your library.

Three features that bloom first — the rest follow.

Library that organizes itself

Folder-watcher hashes mtimes, scanner parses S01E02 / (2020) titles. Add a file, and it appears on the shelf within moments.

SyncPlay across the room or across the country

Weighted-mean NTP offset over 5 samples keeps every device locked to the same frame. Play, pause, seek — everyone moves together.

Phlix Hub — reach any of your servers from anywhere

Sign in once. Reverse-tunnel relay handles NAT. Self-hostable, or use the public hub.

Why Phlix

Seven reasons to bloom.

  • 100% self-hostable — your library never leaves your hardware unless you say so
  • Native clients on Roku, Samsung Tizen, Windows, Mobile, plus any DLNA device
  • Real-time SyncPlay with NTP-style time sync so movie night actually stays in sync
  • Multi-source metadata: TMDB, TVDB, Fanart.tv, and local NFO with 24-hour cache
  • Adaptive HLS streaming, FFmpeg transcoding, per-device quality profiles
  • Live TV with DVR + EPG guide integration
  • Plugin system with a versioned manifest contract

Ready to grow?

Plant your first seed.

One line on a fresh Ubuntu or Debian host. The garden begins to grow the moment you press enter.

One line, on a fresh Ubuntu or Debian host
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/detain/phlix-server/master/scripts/install.sh | sudo bash
Installs PHP 8.3+, MySQL, ffmpeg, creates the database, and starts the service on port 8096.
