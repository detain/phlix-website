Descending: A Phlix Installation Parable

What it means to establish a self-hosted media presence in the deep places. This is the archive's own account of the descent.

The First Descent

There comes a moment in the life of every collector when the accumulated media — scattered across drives, partitions, devices that no longer speak to one another — demands a home of its own. Not a folder. Not a convenient directory. A true archive: indexed, catalogued, accessible from any device in any room, and answerable to no one but its keeper.

The descent begins with a single line. One invocation, issued to a fresh host running Ubuntu or Debian. From that line, the archive takes shape — not merely installed, but conjured into being. The install script consults the system, prepares the database, erects the service, and establishes the first sealed chamber of what will become a vast and observationally complete record of everything you choose to place within it.

The Catalogue

The archive does not merely store. It observes. Each file added to the library is examined, parsed, and entered into the ledger with precise notation. The folder-watcher monitors for changes; the scanner parses S01E02 episode designations and (2020) film-year conventions; the ItemRepository retrieves and hydrates the metadata record from multiple sources simultaneously — TMDB, TVDB, Fanart.tv — each offering a different angle of observation before the archive settles on its final record.

A 24-hour cache ensures the archive does not exhaust its sources in pursuit of completeness. What it has catalogued, it remembers. What it has observed once, it can observe again without renewed consultation. The record is persistent. The notation is exact. Every item in your collection receives the attention its significance warrants.

The Watchers

The archive does not exist in isolation. It must be witnessed — and the witnessing requires vessels suited to the task. Phlix provides native channels for Roku, Samsung Tizen, Windows, and mobile (iOS and Android), each a different manifestation of the same essential observation.

The Roku channel offers HLS playback, Hub mode, and Skip intro/outro annotations. The Samsung Tizen application runs on vanilla JavaScript and webpack, optimised for direct play where the client permits and HLS transcoding where it does not. The Windows desktop client — built on Electron, React, and TypeScript — adds system tray residence and media key support for those who prefer keyboard command. Mobile delivers Movies, TV, Music, Photos, and offline downloads, while token refresh maintains continuous observation even when the application backgrounds itself.

For devices that require no installation at all, the DLNA protocol provides ContentDirectory and AvTransport services through SSDP discovery — an older, quieter form of communion between the archive and the television already mounted on the wall. The archive reaches devices you may have forgotten you owned.

The Synchronized Vigil

To watch alone is one form of observation. To watch in concert — across the room or across the country — is another entirely. SyncPlay maintains a weighted-mean NTP offset across five temporal samples, locking every connected device to the same frame. Play, pause, seek: the room moves as one. The film begins together and ends together, because the archive ensures that time itself bends to the collective attention of its watchers.

The Hub extends this synchronized vigil beyond the local network. Through a reverse-tunnel relay — which you may self-host or delegate to the public relay — the archive follows you to a friend's house, a hotel room, any distance the network permits. Sign in once. The tunnel holds. Your library is present wherever you are, observed from wherever you permit it to be observed.

The Adaptive Frame

Not every device observes at the same resolution of detail. The archive accounts for this through adaptive HLS streaming and per-device quality profiles — mobile-low for constrained connections, mobile-high for capable ones, web for the browser, tv-4k for the display that demands the full measure of its resolution. FFmpeg handles the translation, applying CRF 23 or CRF 28 with libx264 or libx265 encoding as the source material and target display require.

Master playlists and variant streams ensure that the archive offers the correct measure to each observer without forcing any observer to receive more than it can process. The transmission is calibrated. The quality is appropriate. The bandwidth is respected.

The Guarded Archive

Access to the archive is not universal. JWT authentication with refresh tokens establishes identity; Argon2ID hashing secures the seals. Up to five profiles per user allow individual viewers to maintain separate observation histories, separate notations, separate records of what they have seen and when. Four- or six-digit PINs restrict access to designated chambers, and rating filters — from G to NC-17 — ensure the archive reveals only what is appropriate to each watcher.

The archive is yours. It does not share what it observes. It does not report your viewing habits to any third party. It does not require an account with anyone but you. Your library never leaves your hardware unless you explicitly say so.

The Extended Record

Beyond the primary archive, further chambers await extension. The Live TV system — ChannelManager, GuideManager, and Recorder — provides scheduled recordings and an EPG guide that does not require navigating seven menus to discover what time the film begins. The plugin system operates on a versioned manifest contract: implement the LifecycleInterface, ship the manifest, drop the plugin into the designated directory. The loader observes it and activates it. The archive grows, but it grows according to your design, not according to a predetermined form.

The Descent Complete

To descend is not to fall. It is to choose — deliberately, ceremoniously, with full knowledge of what the choice entails — to establish something that will outlast the devices that currently compose it. The archive is not a convenience. It is a commitment to the preservation of what you have chosen to keep, rendered accessible to every device you choose to observe it from, sealed against intrusion by every mechanism the craft affords.

The installation is complete. The archive is observing. What you place within it will be recorded, catalogued, and preserved — not because the archive demands your tribute, but because you have chosen to give it a home in the deep places where it belongs.
