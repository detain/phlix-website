Our Story

Pages from the garden journal — why we tend this plot, how the licence protects everyone, and how to join the gardeners.

Philosophy

Why a garden?

There is a garden gate somewhere in England, half-hidden by climbing roses, that opens onto a path leading straight to the best afternoon of your life. Phlix was built for the viewer who wants their media library to feel like home — not curated perfection, but the joyful overflow of a windowsill cluttered with flower pots and well-loved books. Every feature is a different bloom in the garden, each one tended to grow alongside the others.

The goal is simple: make every viewing moment feel as nourishing and unhurried as an afternoon in a cottage garden — abundant, beautiful, and entirely your own.

License

How the garden is shared

Phlix Server and the Hub are MPL-2.0 — open source, and you can build on them commercially. If you modify a Phlix file, that file stays open; anything you add alongside it is yours. The shared libraries, plugins, and clients are MIT so you can build on them freely.

The licence is a feature, not fine print. It means the garden stays open for everyone who tends it — now and in the future.

Contributing

Growing together

Phlix grows in the open on GitHub. Issues, pull requests, plugin ideas, documentation corrections — every form of tending is welcome. You don't need permission to plant something new; just open a PR and the conversation begins.

The plugin system exists partly for this reason: if you want a bloom that isn't in the garden yet, you can plant it yourself without waiting for the main plot to expand.

Gardeners' Questions

Often asked

Is Phlix like Plex / Jellyfin / Emby?
Yes — same job, different stack. Phlix is built in PHP 8.3+ on Workerman, ships with a versioned plugin contract, and includes a hub for accessing remote servers behind NAT without a third-party tunnel.
Do I need to expose my server to the internet?
No. Run Phlix on your LAN and use the Phlix Hub's reverse-tunnel relay to reach it from your phone or Roku at a friend's house. You can self-host the hub, or use the public one.
What formats are supported?
Anything FFmpeg can read. Direct play when the client supports it; transcoded HLS otherwise. Per-device quality profiles pick the right bitrate automatically.
Is there a mobile app?
Yes — React Native, available on iOS and Android. Currently in beta.
Can I write plugins?
Yes. Implement LifecycleInterface, ship a manifest, drop it in the plugins directory. See phlix-plugin-example for the smallest working starter.
