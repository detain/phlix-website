Our Story

A few pages torn from the garden journal: why this was planted, what the licence lets you do with it, and how to help it grow. The gardeners’ questions are at the end.

The garden journal

Entry one · philosophy

Why we planted it

A media library should feel like a room in your house, not a room in someone else's building. Phlix is self-hostable from top to bottom: the server, the hub, the clients. Your library never leaves your hardware unless you say so.

It is written in PHP 8.3+ on Workerman — an unfashionable choice that turns out to be a warm one. Async where it matters, plain where it does not, and readable enough that you could actually change it.

Entry two · licence

What you may do with it

Phlix Server and the Hub are MPL-2.0 — open source, and you can build on them commercially. If you modify a Phlix file, that file stays open; anything you add alongside it is yours. The shared libraries, plugins, and clients are MIT so you can build on them freely.

Entry three · contributing

How to help it grow

Everything lives in the detain organisation on GitHub — server, hub, shared libraries, docs, the clients, and the plugin example. Issues are the front gate; pull requests are very welcome.

Asked across the fence

Gardeners' Questions

The garden answers patiently, and always the same way twice. Some questions get asked in two different ways — both are here, sharing one answer.

Is Phlix like Plex / Jellyfin / Emby?
Yes — same job, different stack. Phlix is built in PHP 8.3+ on Workerman, ships with a versioned plugin contract, and includes a hub for accessing remote servers behind NAT without a third-party tunnel.
Do I need to expose my server to the internet?
Can I grow this in the cloud, or does it need home soil?
No. Run Phlix on your LAN and use the Phlix Hub's reverse-tunnel relay to reach it from your phone or Roku at a friend's house. You can self-host the hub, or use the public one.
What formats are supported?
Will my old TV in the den be happy here?
Anything FFmpeg can read. Direct play when the client supports it; transcoded HLS otherwise. Per-device quality profiles pick the right bitrate automatically.
Is there a mobile app?
Yes — React Native, available on iOS and Android. Currently in beta.
Can I write plugins?
What if I want to build a wall of my own?
Yes. Implement LifecycleInterface, ship a manifest, drop it in the plugins directory. See phlix-plugin-example for the smallest working starter.
What's the license?
Phlix Server and the Hub are MPL-2.0 — open source, and you can build on them commercially. If you modify a Phlix file, that file stays open; anything you add alongside it is yours. The shared libraries, plugins, and clients are MIT so you can build on them freely.
